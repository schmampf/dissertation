%=========================================================
\chapter{SSN Hybrid Devices} 
\label{ch:ssn-set}
%=========================================================

    This chapter presents transport measurements on two hybrid devices in an SSN configuration. Here, SSN denotes the sequence of a superconducting lead, a superconducting island, and a normal conducting lead. Each device combined a fixed Al--AlO\textsubscript{x}--Cu tunnel barrier with an Al mechanically controllable break junction. Opening the break junction created the second junction and geometrically defined the superconducting island between the two contacts. Although SET1 and SET2 shared the same nominal design and were fabricated in the same production batch, they developed distinct transport regimes after opening. SET1 formed a gate controlled single-electron transistor, whereas SET2 retained a high-conductance contact and exhibited a gate independent zero bias anomaly.

    I fabricated and measured SET1\footnote{In the lab book, SET1 is called `OI-22a-14'.}, shown in Fig.~\ref{fig:ssn-set:sample}, and SET2. Both devices came from the same production batch and used the same nominal design and process parameters. The Al layer was \qty{40}{\nano\meter} thick, and the Cu top layer was approximately \qty{80}{\nano\meter} thick. The oxidation was performed at \qty{4.9(1)}{\milli\barunit} for 2:30\,min. The SSN architecture and the specific design used for these devices originated from the preceding work of Laura Sobral Rey \cite{sobral_rey_interplay_2022}, who introduced me to the design at the beginning of this work. I used this design without adaptation. The fabrication principles and general design considerations are described in Sec.~\ref{section:sample-preparation}.

    Each device provided only one mechanically formed contact configuration suitable for the measurements reported here and failed afterward. Repeated contact configurations and systematic comparisons across contact parameters were therefore not available. The comparison establishes two experimentally observed outcomes of the nominal design, but it does not determine their statistical prevalence or reproducibility across devices.

    I performed these measurements on the Scheer~II setup, which had been developed and operated in the preceding work of Thomas Lorenz, Susanne Sprenger, and Laura Sobral Rey. Detailed descriptions of the platform are given in their theses \cite{lorenz_wechselspiel_2018,sprenger_interplay_2019,sobral_rey_interplay_2022}. Laura Sobral Rey introduced me to the operation of the cryostat, after which I operated the setup independently for these measurements. Before the measurement campaign presented here, I repaired the cryogenic system with valuable technical input from Thomas Lorenz.

    I performed the complete numerical evaluation and model fitting presented here. This included the SIN reference fits, the orthodox normal-state SET fit, the analysis of its structured residuals, and the dynamical Coulomb blockade fit. The data treatment followed the procedure described in Sec.~\ref{sec:methods:digital} and used the Python based analysis pipeline that I developed. The physical interpretation benefited from discussions with Lukas Kammermeier.

    Unless stated otherwise, the parenthetical uncertainties of directly fitted parameters are standard errors obtained from the least squares covariance matrix. They quantify the local statistical uncertainty within the respective model and do not include systematic uncertainty from calibration, contact drift, or model choice. I estimated the bath temperature uncertainties as \qty{1}{\milli\kelvin}.

    Section~\ref{sec:ssn-set:static} first establishes the intact tunnel barrier of SET1 and then examines the normal and superconducting transport after opening the break junction. In the normal-state SET, the gate--bias response reveals Coulomb diamonds, and an orthodox SET fit provides the minimal electrostatic description. I additionally report the structured residuals that remained after this fit. In the superconducting SSN SET, I report gate dependent superconducting switching as a separate experimental outcome.

    Section~\ref{sec:ssn-set:dynamic} presents SET2 as a contrasting outcome of the same device concept. No gate modulation was resolved after the break junction was partially opened. Instead, the normal-state response was consistent with dynamical Coulomb blockade, and the superconducting response developed two conductance regimes separated by a crossover. The analysis compares the independently extracted resistance and energy scales while keeping the microscopic origin of their correspondence open.
