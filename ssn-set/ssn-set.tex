\chapter{SSN Hybrid Devices} 
\label{ch:ssn-set}
%=========================================================

    This chapter presents transport measurements on two hybrid devices in an SSN configuration. Here, SSN denotes the sequence of a superconducting lead, a superconducting island, and a normal conducting lead. Each device combines a fixed Al--AlO\textsubscript{x}--Cu tunnel barrier with an Al mechanically controllable break junction. Opening the break junction creates the second junction and geometrically defines the superconducting island between the two contacts. Although SET1 and SET2 share the same nominal design and are fabricated in the same production batch, they develop distinct transport regimes after opening. SET1 forms a gate controlled single-electron transistor, whereas SET2 retains a high-conductance contact and exhibits a gate independent zero bias anomaly.

    I fabricate and measure SET1, shown in Fig.~\ref{fig:ssn-set:sample}, and SET2. Both devices come from the same production batch and use the same nominal design and process parameters. The Al layer is \qty{40}{\nano\meter} thick, and the Cu top layer is approximately \qty{80}{\nano\meter} thick. I perform the oxidation at \qty{4.9(1)}{\milli\barunit} for 2:30\,min. The SSN architecture and the specific design used for these devices originate from the preceding work of Laura Sobral Rey \cite{sobral_rey_interplay_2022}, who introduces me to the design at the beginning of this work. I use this design without adaptation. The fabrication principles and general design considerations are described in Sec.~\ref{section:sample-preparation}.

    Each device provides only one mechanically formed contact configuration suitable for the measurements reported here and fails afterward. Repeated contact configurations and systematic comparisons across contact parameters are therefore not available. The comparison establishes two experimentally observed outcomes of the nominal design, but it does not determine their statistical prevalence or reproducibility across devices.

    I perform these measurements on the Scheer~II setup, which had been developed and operated in the preceding work of Thomas Lorenz, Susanne Sprenger, and Laura Sobral Rey. Detailed descriptions of the platform are given in their theses \cite{lorenz_wechselspiel_2018,sprenger_interplay_2019,sobral_rey_interplay_2022}. Laura Sobral Rey introduces me to the operation of the cryostat, after which I operate the setup independently for these measurements. Before the measurement campaign presented here, I repair the cryogenic system with valuable technical input from Thomas Lorenz.

    I perform the complete numerical evaluation and model fitting presented here. This includes the SIN reference fits, the orthodox normal-state SET fit, the analysis of its structured residuals, and the dynamical Coulomb blockade fit. The data treatment follows the procedure described in Sec.~\ref{sec:methods:digital} and uses the Python based analysis pipeline that I develop. The physical interpretation benefits from discussions with Lukas Kammermeier.

    Unless stated otherwise, the parenthetical uncertainties of directly fitted parameters are standard errors obtained from the least squares covariance matrix. They quantify the local statistical uncertainty within the respective model and do not include systematic uncertainty from calibration, contact drift, or model choice. I estimate the bath temperature uncertainties as \qty{1}{\milli\kelvin}.

    Section~\ref{sec:ssn-set:static} first establishes the intact tunnel barrier of SET1 and then examines the normal and superconducting transport after opening the break junction. In the normal-state SET, the gate--bias response reveals Coulomb diamonds, and an orthodox SET fit provides the minimal electrostatic description. I additionally report the structured residuals that remain after this fit. In the superconducting SSN SET, I report gate dependent superconducting switching as a separate experimental outcome.

    Section~\ref{sec:ssn-set:dynamic} presents SET2 as a contrasting outcome of the same device concept. After partially opening the break junction, I do not resolve any gate modulation. Instead, the normal-state response is consistent with dynamical Coulomb blockade, and the superconducting response develops two conductance regimes separated by a crossover. The analysis compares the independently extracted resistance and energy scales while keeping the microscopic origin of their correspondence open.
