\chapter{Experimental Results} 
\label{chapter:experimental-results}
%=========================================================

    This chapter presents the experimental results on superconducting charge transport in nanostructures with progressively increasing transmission. The measurements span the tunnel regime, in which quasiparticle transport provides a direct probe of the superconducting density of states, and atomic-scale contacts, in which transport is governed by a small number of channels with finite transmission. Microwave irradiation introduces an additional control parameter and allows us to examine photon assisted, nonlinear, and nonequilibrium transport across these regimes.

    Throughout the chapter, I characterize the devices using measured \textit{I--V} curves and the numerically derived \textit{dI--dV} and \textit{dV--dI} responses. I compare these observables with minimal transport models appropriate to each regime. The models provide reference responses, support the extraction of experimentally relevant parameters, and identify conditions under which the underlying assumptions cease to describe the measurements. Particular attention is given to microwave calibration, spectral resolution, reproducibility, and the distinction between effects intrinsic to the device and those associated with its electromagnetic environment.
