%=========================================================

    % most broad statement on importance of computers
    % scaleability and Moore's Law
    Computation now underlies science, industry, and everyday life. As the scope and scale of computation expand, so does the demand for processing power. Meeting that demand requires more energy \cite{iea_energy_ai_2025}. For decades, computing advanced to the rhythm of Moore's law. Smaller transistors placed ever more functionality on each chip, while reductions in operating voltage limited the accompanying increase in power consumption \cite{moore_cramming_1965,dennard_design_1974}. This strategy no longer guarantees proportional improvements in performance and energy efficiency because power dissipation, fabrication complexity, material properties, and device physics make further scaling increasingly difficult \cite{markov_limits_2014}.
    
    % quantum computers are the shit
    As conventional scaling approaches its practical limits, quantum mechanics offers a way out, an entirely new basis for computation. A sufficiently powerful quantum computer can already solve selected problems beyond the practical reach of classical machines, as demonstrated by a verifiable simulation \cite{google_quantum_ai_ergodicity_2025}. Such simulations could transform how molecules, pharmaceuticals, catalysts, and other quantum materials are understood and designed \cite{feynman_simulating_1982,mcardle_quantum_2020}. Other algorithms could break widely used public key cryptosystems, forcing a fundamental change in how digital communication is secured \cite{shor_algorithms_1994}. These machines may not replace classical computers, but their impact on science, industry, and everyday life could be revolutionary.

    % super conducting qpits are the most promising quantum computer design
    % Limits of superconducting Qbits
    Superconducting circuits form the hardware basis of several of the world's most advanced quantum processors and mark a principal route toward scalable quantum computing \cite{devoret_superconducting_2013,kjaergaard_superconducting_2020,arute_quantum_2019}. Their fast operation, lithographic fabrication, and compatibility with integrated microwave control have enabled increasingly complex processors and major advances in quantum error correction \cite{google_quantum_ai_error_2025}. These processors rely on Josephson junctions, typically formed by two superconducting electrodes separated by an ultrathin insulating barrier. Their nonlinearity enables qubit operation, while their dissipation and microwave response originate from microscopic charge transport. The same scalability that makes superconducting circuits attractive also exposes their microscopic limitations. Nonequilibrium quasiparticles tunneling across a Josephson junction can relax the qubit, while defects in amorphous tunnel barriers absorb energy and produce fluctuations in relaxation time \cite{catelani_relaxation_2011,klimov_fluctuations_2018,bilmes_probing_2022}. As processors grow, understanding and controlling these local loss channels becomes essential to their reproducibility and performance.

    \newpage
    % establish the experimental strategy
    % establish the microwave probe
    Reaching that microscopic understanding requires studying a single junction in isolation, apart from the full qubit circuit whose collective response can hide individual transport processes. I compare two complementary junction regimes for that purpose. Oxide tunnel barriers set the weak transmission limit, while atomic contacts expose transport through discrete channels of finite and high transmission \cite{scheer_conduction_1997,agrait_quantum_2003}. This controlled progression tests where an effective circuit description remains sufficient and where individual charge transfer processes must be resolved. Microwave irradiation extends this comparison into the driven regime. The spacing and amplitude evolution of the resulting structures reveal transferred charges and overlapping transport processes, directly testing transport descriptions under drive.

    % establish the methods
    In this thesis, I investigate aluminum junctions at millikelvin temperatures under dc bias and microwave irradiation. Oxide tunnel barriers serve as the weak transmission reference, while a mechanically controllable break junction forms atomic contacts with different channel compositions. Static spectra establish the superconducting energy scales and channel transmissions. Driven spectra then reveal how quasiparticle, multiple Andreev reflection (MAR), and Cooper pair contributions evolve with microwave amplitude and transmission.

    %=========================================================
    \subsubsection*{State of the Art and Open Questions}
    %=========================================================

    % static transport
    Static transport offers a well-established reference for driven measurements. In the tunnel limit, single-particle tunneling is elastic, and the current arises from electrons tunneling between the two BCS densities of states, weighted by their thermal occupation. Because each density of states diverges at the gap edge, the \textit{I--V} characteristic develops a sharp current onset once the bias voltage reaches the sum of the two superconducting gaps, and this quasiparticle peak directly maps the size of the gap \cite{bardeen_tunnelling_1961,giaever_study_1961}. As the barrier transmission increases, an incident quasiparticle can undergo multiple Andreev reflections at the two interfaces before finally crossing the junction. Each reflection converts the quasiparticle between electron and hole character and lets it pick up an additional increment of bias energy, so that a transport process of order \(m\) turns on once the bias voltage reaches \(eV = 2\Delta/m\). This mechanism drives current at subgap bias and produces a series of conductance features known as the subharmonic gap structure \cite{averin_ac_1995,cuevas_hamiltonian_1996}. Because the amplitude of each order depends sensitively on the channel transmission, fitting the complete subgap \textit{I--V} characteristic with multichannel scattering theory extracts the discrete channel transmissions of an atomic contact \cite{scheer_conduction_1997,agrait_quantum_2003}.

    % Josephson and environment
    Coherent Cooper pair transport gives a complementary macroscopic description of the same junction. Cooper pairs tunnel without dissipation, and the resulting supercurrent follows the Josephson relations, which set the current by the sine of the superconducting phase difference and set the time evolution of that phase by the bias voltage. Embedding this nonlinear, phase dependent element in a parallel resistance and capacitance, the RCSJ model reproduces the classical current--voltage dynamics of a real junction, including the hysteresis between its switching and retrapping branches \cite{josephson_possible_1962,stewart_currentvoltage_1968,mccumber_effect_1968}. A junction cannot generally be treated in isolation from this circuit environment, even under static bias. Each tunneling event, whether it transfers a quasiparticle or a Cooper pair, can exchange energy with the electromagnetic modes of the surrounding capacitance and impedance, and this exchange reshapes the measured \textit{I--V} response near the gap and near zero bias. In the tunnel limit, \textit{P(E)} Theory captures this effect by convolving the bare tunneling rate with the probability that the environment absorbs or supplies a given energy during the tunneling event \cite{falci_quasiparticle_1991,ingold_charge_1992}.

    % addind microwaves
    Microwave driving adds coherent and incoherent responses to this environment dependent baseline. In the tunnel limit, the oscillating drive periodically shifts the electrochemical potential of the electrodes, so a tunneling quasiparticle can absorb or emit one or more photon quanta during the tunneling process. The Tien-Gordon model describes the resulting current as a sum of energy shifted copies of the static quasiparticle current, weighted by the probability of exchanging a given number of photons, and these copies appear as replicas displaced from the static onset by integer multiples of the photon energy \cite{tien_multiphoton_1963}. Coherent Cooper pair tunneling responds differently. Under dc bias, the Josephson relations already predict an oscillating supercurrent at a frequency set by the bias voltage, and when this oscillation locks to the microwave drive, the phase advances by a fixed number of cycles per drive period. This phase locking pins the dc voltage to a comb of values set by the drive frequency, which appear as Shapiro steps in the \textit{I--V} curve \cite{shapiro_josephson_1963}. At finite transmission, the same photon exchange also acts on the Andreev reflection process, so each MAR order can additionally absorb or emit photons, and photon assisted multiple Andreev reflection (PAMAR) shifts the static charge order thresholds into a comb of finite-bias sidebands \cite{cuevas_subharmonic_2002}. Cooper pairs can also tunnel incoherently under drive, absorbing photons without the running phase that Shapiro steps require, and this photon assisted incoherent Cooper pair tunneling (PAICPT) produces its own replica pattern near zero bias, distinct because it does not depend on phase locking \cite{roychowdhury_microwave_2015}. These mechanisms can overlap in the same spectrum, making their separation a central interpretive problem.

    % chauvin et al
    Voltage biased aluminum atomic contacts form the closest experimental match. Static MAR yields the channel transmissions, while microwave spectra show coherent integer and fractional Shapiro resonances on a PAMAR background. The low-impedance environment supports this phase locking, and a microscopic driven MAR theory reproduces representative finite-bias conductance traces using the measured transmissions and calibrated drive without adjustable parameters \cite{chauvin_superconducting_2006}. These measurements establish quantitative PAMAR, but do not address its amplitude resolved separation from incoherent Cooper pair transport.

    % STMs et al.
    % Carrad et al.
    Josephson STM experiments identify PAICPT through its replica spacing and squared-Bessel amplitude dependence \cite{roychowdhury_microwave_2015}. Across different STM conductance regimes, quasiparticle tunneling, PAICPT, and driven MAR appear in the same spectra. Direct charge dependent dressing of the static full counting statistics (FCS) components fails to reproduce the irradiated MAR response, while a microscopic calculation performs better but retains substantial deviations \cite{kot_microwave-assisted_2020}. Related measurements of Yu-Shiba-Rusinov states further show that separate electron and hole thresholds can make the visible sideband spacing differ from the net transferred charge \cite{peters_resonant_2020}. A different platform, gate tunable semiconductor--superconductor nanowire Josephson junctions, reveals charge dependent MAR sidebands in microwave amplitude and frequency maps. A phenomenological comparison dresses the measured static current under the piecewise assumption that one MAR order dominates near each threshold \cite{carrad_photon-assisted_2022}.

    % the Gap
    Taken together, previous studies leave an experimental as well as a modeling gap. They demonstrate individual driven transport mechanisms in selected devices and spectra, but do not systematically map coexisting PAMAR and PAICPT across complete microwave amplitude sweeps and independently characterized contact configurations. The microscopic calculation and the phenomenological comparison above illustrate the resulting trade-off. Full Floquet treatments capture coherent microwave dynamics but are computationally demanding, while phenomenological constructions are easier to apply but rely on measured line shapes and piecewise assignments \cite{kot_microwave-assisted_2020,carrad_photon-assisted_2022}. What is missing is therefore both a systematic comparison across the few-channel regime and a practical description that predicts the driven response from independently characterized static transport.

    %=========================================================
    \subsubsection*{Overview}
    %=========================================================

    In this thesis, I systematically measure microwave amplitude sweeps across atomic contacts with substantially different channel compositions. Each sweep is a series of \textit{I--V} curves recorded at increasing microwave amplitude. I display these data as amplitude maps, with bias and drive amplitude as the two coordinates and current or differential response encoded by color. The data reveal coexisting PAMAR and PAICPT throughout the few-channel regime and establish how their trajectories and spectral weights evolve with transmission and drive. To explain these maps, I develop a deterministic threshold decomposition. It partitions the simulated static MAR conductance around its known thresholds and reconstructs an effective current for each charge order after direct FCS dressing fails across the full maps. Charge dependent photon dressing and an independently constrained PAICPT contribution then reproduce the dominant trajectories and spectral weight redistribution without selecting structures from the driven measurement. Chapter~\ref{ch:superconducting-transport} develops the theory behind this analysis, from BCS quasiparticle tunneling and Josephson phase dynamics to channel resolved MAR and environmental energy exchange. Chapter~\ref{ch:methods} then details the underlying experiment, beginning with sample fabrication. It also covers the cryogenic MCBJ setup, filtering, and the electrical measurement circuit, along with the resulting acquisition, processing, and fitting.

    The static environmental analysis and the zero-bias replicas support incoherent Cooper pair transport rather than coherent Shapiro response. Tests from moderate transmissions to a nearly ballistic leading channel, presented in Section~\ref{sec:atomic-contacts}, show that the combined framework captures the principal few-channel microwave response. The framework acts as a practical model for testing charge assignments and separating overlapping mechanisms. At high conductance, the same data reveal an additional subharmonic photon assisted response, a genuinely new observation whose microscopic origin remains an open question.

    In a complementary tunnel barrier experiment, presented in Section~\ref{sec:tunnelbarrier}, I observe a microwave induced rectification effect that, to my knowledge, has not previously been reported. The local current asymmetry reaches \qty{25}{\percent}, and this strong bias asymmetric response lies beyond the established bias symmetric Tien-Gordon model. The effect occurs within a specific operating range set jointly by voltage bias, microwave amplitude, and frequency. Together, these three control parameters tune the magnitude and polarity of the rectification. Chapter~\ref{ch:conclusion} draws these results together into a unified picture of driven charge transport in aluminum junctions, and the same quasiparticle tunneling, MAR, and Cooper pair processes resolved throughout this thesis govern the dissipation and decoherence that ultimately limit superconducting quantum circuits.
