\section*{Abstract}
Under microwave irradiation, quasiparticle, Cooper pair, and multiple Andreev reflection (MAR) transport overlap in the same spectrum, where position alone no longer identifies a process. I separate them with independent static characterization and complete microwave amplitude maps. Static transport fixes the energy thresholds and the channel composition, the replica spacing sets the transferred charge, and the amplitude evolution distinguishes incoherent tunneling from coherent phase locking. I apply this to aluminum junctions at millikelvin temperatures, from oxide tunnel barriers to atomic contacts in a mechanically controllable break junction, with the local ac drive calibrated in situ.
 
In the first of two tunnel junctions, the Tien-Gordon Model reproduces the complete driven evolution, with individual photon assisted quasiparticle replicas resolved to at least photon order 25. To my knowledge, no previous amplitude map of a superconducting tunnel junction tracks replicas that far. A second, nominally similar junction shows microwave induced rectification reaching a local current asymmetry of \qty{25}{\percent}, tuned in magnitude and polarity by dc bias, microwave amplitude, and frequency, beyond the bias symmetric Tien-Gordon description. Its drive frequency matches an independently identified mode of the sample environment, and a resonantly driven Josephson weak link plausibly explains the threshold, frequency sensitivity, and polarity reversal, although the mechanism remains open.
 
For atomic contacts, channel resolved fits to the static MAR spectra yield the mesoscopic pincode. Along an opening trace they evolve within plateaus and reorganize at abrupt jumps, revealing contact changes hidden in the normal-state conductance. Using the pincode as independent input, I partition the static MAR conductance around its thresholds and dress each order by its charge, giving threshold weighted photon assisted MAR (PAMAR). Combined with photon assisted incoherent Cooper pair tunneling (PAICPT), it reproduces the dominant trajectories and their spectral weight from moderate transmission to a nearly ballistic leading channel, without fitting driven features. The squared-Bessel amplitude evolution of the zero-bias replicas supports PAICPT over coherent Shapiro transport. At the highest conductance the description reaches its limit and a subharmonic PAICPT response appears, with replicas at half the conventional Cooper pair spacing that to my knowledge have not been reported before.
 
Microwave amplitude spectroscopy therefore links the tunnel limit to a practical PAMAR and PAICPT description of driven few-channel transport, and exposes two responses at its limits, a tunable rectification and a subharmonic PAICPT response.
