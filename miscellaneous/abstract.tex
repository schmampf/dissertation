\chapter*{Abstract}
Superconducting junctions support different charge transfer processes depending on their channel transmission and electromagnetic environment. Under microwave irradiation, quasiparticle, Cooper pair, and multiple Andreev reflection transport can overlap within the same spectrum, which complicates their identification. In this thesis, I investigated how microwave driven charge transport evolves from superconducting tunnel barriers to atomic contacts with high channel transmission. I combined millikelvin \textit{I--V} spectroscopy, in situ microwave calibration, static transport modeling, and complete microwave amplitude maps.

In an SIS tunnel junction, I quantitatively reproduced the driven response with the Tien-Gordon Model and continuously resolved quasiparticle replicas to at least photon order 25. This result establishes photon assisted tunneling deep in the multiphoton regime and provides a reference for junctions with more complex transport. In a second tunnel junction, I observed microwave induced rectification with a local current asymmetry of up to \qty{25}{\percent}. Dc bias, microwave amplitude, and frequency jointly controlled its magnitude and polarity. The response exceeded the bias symmetric Tien-Gordon description, while its microscopic mechanism remained unresolved.

For atomic contacts, I extracted the channel transmissions from static multiple Andreev reflection spectra and used them as independent input for the analysis of the driven response. I developed a deterministic threshold decomposition that combines the static multiple Andreev reflection contributions with charge dependent photon dressing. The resulting framework reproduced the dominant finite-bias trajectories and their spectral weight evolution across contacts ranging from moderate transmission to a nearly ballistic leading channel. Static environmental analysis and the amplitude evolution of replicas originating from zero bias supported their assignment to photon assisted incoherent Cooper pair tunneling rather than coherent Shapiro transport. At the highest investigated conductance, I observed an additional replica family at half the conventional Cooper pair spacing. This subharmonic response is consistent with an effective transfer charge of \(4e\), although the measurements do not establish correlated transfer of two Cooper pairs or identify a unique microscopic mechanism.

These results establish microwave amplitude spectroscopy as a charge sensitive method for separating overlapping transport processes through their static thresholds, replica spacing, and amplitude evolution. They connect quantitative photon assisted tunneling in the tunnel limit with a practical description of driven few-channel transport and reveal new responses where microscopic charge transfer and the electromagnetic environment can no longer be treated independently.
