%=========================================================
\chapter{Conclusion}
\label{ch:conclusion}
\thispagestyle{chapterstart}
%=========================================================

    In this thesis, I systematically map microwave driven superconducting transport from the tunnel limit to atomic contacts with nearly perfect channel transmission. Across this range, the same experimental strategy connects static junction characterization with complete microwave amplitude maps. This progression resolves conventional photon assisted tunneling with unusually high resolution and establishes a unified phenomenological framework for photon assisted few-channel transport. Along the way, it reveals two previously unreported responses, a tunable microwave induced rectification and a subharmonic photon assisted incoherent Cooper pair tunneling (PAICPT) response at half the conventional replica spacing.

    Static transport determines the superconducting energy scales and, for atomic contacts, the mesoscopic pincode. Microwave irradiation then introduces a second spectroscopic dimension. The voltage positions of the resulting structures constrain their energy thresholds and transferred charges, while their evolution with drive amplitude distinguishes processes that occupy the same voltage range. This combination transforms complex transport maps into a systematic comparison of quasiparticle, Cooper pair, and multiple Andreev reflection (MAR) transport.

    Stable mechanically controllable break junctions and a common measurement and analysis chain make the comparison possible across all regimes. In situ calibration converts the hardware dependent microwave output into the local ac voltage experienced by each junction, so the maps can be compared on the physical drive coordinate of the transport models.

    %=========================================================
    \subsubsection*{From Tunnel Barriers to Atomic Contacts}
    %=========================================================

        The first tunnel junction establishes the high-resolution tunnel reference. Its static transport fits the SIS BCS model, and I reproduce its complete microwave driven evolution with the Tien-Gordon model to exceptional agreement. Individual photon assisted replicas remain visually resolved and can be followed continuously to at least order 25. The measured ladder extends substantially beyond the superconducting gap while retaining the predicted redistribution of spectral weight across the full amplitude range. To my knowledge, no previous SIS amplitude map has continuously resolved quasiparticle replicas to a higher order. This result demonstrates quantitative Tien-Gordon transport deep into the multiphoton regime and sets the reference against which the more complex junctions can be understood.

        A second, nominally similar tunnel junction reveals the first new phenomenon. I observe microwave induced rectification with a local current asymmetry reaching up to \qty{25}{\percent}. Dc bias, microwave amplitude, and frequency jointly control its magnitude and polarity, switching it on with increasing drive and reversing its direction across the investigated interval. The response is nonhysteretic and turns the junction into a microwave controlled diode-like element. Comparison with the bias symmetric Tien-Gordon response isolates the additional asymmetric contribution and establishes a regime beyond uniform ac driving. Static magnetic field maps show no unexpected features and the transport stays unchanged across a wide range of gate voltages, supporting a microwave induced origin over a static flux sensitive or Coulomb blockaded state. The narrow drive frequency at which the effect appears also matches an electromagnetic mode of the sample environment identified independently in an earlier transmission measurement of the same cryostat. A resonantly driven Josephson weak link, consistent with the conventional switching current of a comparable junction, provides a plausible explanation for the drive threshold, frequency sensitivity, and polarity reversal.

        Moving from the tunnel regime to atomic contacts introduces discrete channels of finite and nearly perfect transmission, and with them the question of how to separate overlapping transport processes when several channels contribute at once. Static characterization along a complete opening trace shows that the extracted pincodes evolve continuously within each conductance plateau and reorganize at abrupt jumps, revealing contact changes that stay hidden in the normal-state conductance alone. Under microwave irradiation, the same channels support quasiparticle, MAR, and Cooper pair processes that overlap in the same voltage range, so the static pincode alone can no longer explain the driven maps. To separate these processes, I decompose the static MAR response deterministically around its known energy thresholds and apply charge dependent photon dressing to each resulting order. Combined with PAICPT, this framework reproduces the dominant trajectories and their spectral weight evolution across complete amplitude maps, without adjusting parameters to fit individual driven features.

        To test the generality of this description, I move beyond the single contact where I develop it. Testing it across contacts with substantially different pincodes, from moderate transmission to a nearly ballistic leading channel, shows that threshold weighted PAMAR captures the dominant finite-bias response throughout this range, while the squared-Bessel amplitude evolution of the replicas originating from zero bias supports their assignment to PAICPT rather than to a coherent Shapiro response. The model therefore does more than reproduce a collection of lines. It assigns overlapping structures consistently to their energy thresholds, effective charge orders, and static transport origins, across contacts that share little beyond the same measurement and analysis chain.

        At the highest conductance investigated, a second new phenomenon emerges, a subharmonic PAICPT response at half the conventional replica spacing. To my knowledge, no earlier measurement has reported this response. In this contact, the microwave maps contain trajectories running halfway between the conventional Cooper pair replicas, at half their spacing in voltage, positions that no combination of the established single-pair replicas can reach. A second, distinct replica family at half the conventional spacing is therefore required to describe them. Within the phenomenological construction, this spacing matches the effective transfer of two Cooper pairs, placing the response one step beyond the incoherent single-pair transport that describes the other contacts. Their squared-Bessel-like amplitude evolution is consistent with this assignment, but does not by itself establish a simultaneous, correlated two-pair transfer or identify the microscopic process behind it. The replica pattern is a new result in its own right, independent of this unresolved microscopic interpretation. It marks the point where the controlled few-channel description reaches its limit, opening a new region in which higher charge correlations, phase dynamics, and the electromagnetic environment can be investigated together.

    %=========================================================
    \subsubsection*{From Microwave Response to Transport Processes}
    %=========================================================

        Microwave maps become interpretable transport data only when combined with three complementary coordinates. Feature spacing alone is insufficient when quasiparticle, MAR, and Cooper pair structures overlap. Static transport supplies the channel composition and energy thresholds, microwave spacing determines the transferred charge, and amplitude evolution distinguishes incoherent tunneling from coherent phase locking. Together, these coordinates separate coexisting processes by their static origin, charge, and coherence without selecting isolated features from the driven data.

        This perspective also clarifies when a macroscopic junction description is sufficient and when microscopic charge processes must be resolved. In the tunnel reference, a single driven voltage and the static \textit{I--V} characteristic capture the response over an exceptionally wide range. At finite transmission, the individual channels and voltage dependent MAR charge orders become essential. The electromagnetic environment adds another active degree of freedom by setting the local drive, enabling incoherent Cooper pair transfer, and coupling phase dynamics back into quasiparticle transport. The rectification in the second tunnel junction and the subharmonic response in the highest-conductance atomic contact appear precisely where these additional degrees of freedom become visible. They therefore extend the systematic progression rather than standing as unrelated anomalies.

        The distinction between circuit parameters and microscopic transport has direct implications for superconducting quantum circuits. Their Josephson junctions are commonly represented by a few circuit quantities, although dissipation and microwave response originate from charge transport within a local electromagnetic environment. The measurements here show that this environment can set the junction drive, redistribute quasiparticle and Cooper pair transport, and generate frequency dependent responses absent from static characterization. Dedicated test structures based on the same spectroscopy could probe microwave delivery, resonances of the sample holder and wiring, subgap transport, and unintended high-transmission channels. They would not measure qubit coherence directly, but could reveal how junction fabrication and the surrounding microwave circuit influence microscopic dissipative processes that limit circuit reproducibility.

        The underlying approach is not restricted to superconducting qubits or metallic atomic contacts. Hybrid nanowires, quantum dots, molecular junctions, superconducting detectors, and driven single-charge devices all support regimes in which several charge transfer processes coexist under periodic drive. The same three coordinates apply there, provided static transport independently fixes the relevant energy thresholds, as the pincode does here, so that the microwave spacing and amplitude evolution can be compared against a prediction rather than fitted freely. Where that condition holds, they separate coexisting processes by their static origin, charge, and coherence in these systems too, not only in metallic atomic contacts. Where it does not, for example when the relevant thresholds are not independently accessible or several channels remain unresolved, the same three coordinates cannot separate coexisting processes, and a different characterization step would have to substitute for the pincode used here.

        The framework remains phenomenological, but independently characterized static transport constrains its physical assignments. It bridges the transparent Tien-Gordon limit and a microscopic nonequilibrium treatment, providing a benchmark for determining which features require coherence, occupation dynamics, or environmental backaction.

    %=========================================================
    \subsubsection*{Future Research}
    %=========================================================

        The rectification found in the second tunnel junction motivates a dedicated follow-up rather than a side test. A deliberately fabricated series of tunnel junctions with systematically varied circuit asymmetry, for example in sample position within the shielding environment, lead impedance, or electrical path length, would turn the uncontrolled asymmetry of this sample into a controlled parameter and map how the drive threshold, frequency sensitivity, and polarity reversal scale with it. Combined with the frequency and amplitude sweeps already established here, this would test whether the effect scales as expected for a resonantly driven Josephson weak link, and could turn a favorable asymmetry into a designed microwave rectifier rather than an observed one. This step needs no new measurement technique and only variations on the existing sample fabrication, making it a natural first project on its own.

        The next step is to embed the junction in a deliberately designed electromagnetic environment. A calibrated impedance, resonator, or superconducting loop would turn microwave amplitude, phase, and mode structure into controlled variables, extending the asymmetry series above with an independent check of the resonantly driven weak link picture. The same control addresses a broader question in the atomic contacts, separating incoherent Cooper pair transfer from Josephson phase locking by making the environment an experimental variable rather than a fixed backdrop. A tunable weak link embedded in a resonator could additionally correlate charge transfer and photon exchange with resonator loss, quasiparticle dynamics, or qubit relaxation, so that the environment becomes a controlled quantum degree of freedom rather than an uncontrolled boundary condition.

        The subharmonic response, observed in a single contact reached after reclosing the break junction, calls for the same systematic mapping already applied across the rest of the few-channel regime, and reaches further into open theoretical territory. A series of highly transmissive contacts, tuned deliberately into and across the conductance range where the subharmonic replicas appear, would establish how the effect depends on transmission, channel number, and drive amplitude. Finite-frequency current noise would add an independent, charge sensitive probe alongside the amplitude maps, since a genuinely correlated two-pair transfer and two uncorrelated single-pair events at the same average current would leave distinct signatures in the noise. Microscopic Floquet or Keldysh calculations can build directly on the extracted pincodes, calibrated drives, and charge order assignments to determine which structures follow from charge quantization and static thresholds and which require coherent interference, nonequilibrium occupations, or environmental backaction, settling in particular whether the subharmonic response reflects higher charge correlations or a more conventional process appearing at an unexpected spacing.

        A superconducting single-electron transistor provides the natural next platform beyond this. It adds charging energy and gate induced charge to the controls established here. The connection between MAR charge order and energy threshold offers a way to organize its dense stability diagrams. Gate--bias--amplitude maps could follow how a process transferring multiple charges crosses a Coulomb threshold, exchanges photons, and changes the island charge, while phase control distinguishes coherent Josephson cycles from incoherent quasiparticle and MAR sequences. This extends the present framework toward the joint study of superconducting coherence, nonequilibrium, and charge fluctuations.

    %=========================================================
    \subsubsection*{Final Remarks}
    %=========================================================

        Systematic microwave amplitude spectroscopy connects superconducting transport across widely different transmission regimes. It resolves the Tien-Gordon response with exceptional dynamic range, relates finite-transmission structures to their energy thresholds and effective charges, and reveals microwave controlled rectification and a subharmonic response at the boundaries of these descriptions. These results establish a basis for experiments with controlled phase, impedance, and island charge.

        Superconducting quantum technologies ultimately depend on controlling how charge and phase respond to electromagnetic fields at individual junctions. By turning these microscopic responses into experimentally distinguishable signatures, the measurements presented here contribute to the understanding needed to make superconducting circuits more controllable, reproducible, and scalable.
