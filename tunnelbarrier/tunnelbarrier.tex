\section{Tunnel Barrier}
\label{sec:tunnelbarrier}
%=========================================================

    Superconducting tunnel barriers provide a access to the quasiparticle density of states. Their response under microwave irradiation additionally reveals photon assisted tunneling through features separated by the photon energy. These devices therefore provide a controlled platform for testing the SIS transport model, examining the limits of the Tien--Gordon description, and calibrating the microwave amplitude experienced by a nanoscale junction.

    In this chapter, I investigate static and microwave driven quasiparticle transport in two nominally similar aluminum tunnel junctions, denoted TB1 and TB2. The central questions are whether their static transport can be described consistently by BCS tunneling, whether the irradiated response follows bias symmetric photon assisted tunneling, and whether the effective microwave amplitude can be extracted from the measured transport. Comparing two devices also tests which features are reproducible and which depend on the junction or its electromagnetic environment.

    I measured TB2 first using an earlier version of the measurement setup. I subsequently measured TB1 with improved filtering and higher spectral resolution. Both devices contain an aluminum tunnel barrier, but their fabrication and local circuit environments are not identical. In particular, an additional shadow electrode and break junction path remained connected in TB2. This distinction provides important context for the different microwave responses observed in the two devices.

    The analysis uses the measured \textit{I--V} curves together with the numerically derived \textit{dI--dV} and \textit{dV--dI} responses. I first extract static junction parameters from the SIS BCS model and then compare the irradiated transport with the Tien--Gordon description. Where the measured response remains bias symmetric, the fitted amplitude $A_\mathrm{fit}$ provides an effective calibration of the microwave drive. When strong rectification develops, the symmetry assumption of the model fails and $A_\mathrm{fit}$ no longer represents a unique physical microwave amplitude.

    The results progress from the conventional response of TB1 to the unexpected behavior of TB2. TB1 exhibits well resolved, bias symmetric photon assisted quasiparticle transport and establishes the reference behavior of the junctions. TB2 shows the same basic superconducting tunneling characteristics but develops a pronounced microwave induced bias asymmetry within a narrow frequency interval. The asymmetry increases above a drive threshold and reverses direction with frequency. The measurements establish this evolution and test its correlations with microwave coupling and heating, but they do not determine a unique microscopic mechanism.
