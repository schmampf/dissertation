    %=========================================================
    \subsection{The Mesoscopic Pincode}
    \label{sec:ac:pincode}
    %=========================================================

    The objective of this section is to reproduce the channel-resolved analysis introduced by Scheer et al. for superconducting aluminum atomic contacts \cite{scheer_conduction_1997}. I formed atomic contacts with a mechanically controlled break junction, measured their nonlinear superconducting \textit{I--V} characteristics, and fitted the multiple-Andreev-reflection spectrum as a sum of independent transport channels. This procedure assigns a mesoscopic pincode \(\{\tau_i\}\) to each stable contact configuration. I first validate the extraction for representative spectra and then use it to follow the individual transmission channels during the opening of the contact.
    
    The mesoscopic pincode \(\{\tau_i\}\) describes the transmission eigenvalues of the atomic contact, as introduced in Sec.~\ref{subsec:quasi:meso}. Its channel-dependent MAR spectrum is discussed in Sec.~\ref{subsec:meso:mar}. I extracted the pincode using the fitting procedure described in Sec.~\ref{subsec:methods:digital:analysis}.

    After characterization of the unbroken sample, the break junction can be broken and atomic contacts might form. By measuring spectra over small changes in displacements, one can obtain the mesoscopic pincode.

    Figure \ref{fig:ac:pincode:fits} shows representative fits of taken I--V spectra at positions $\Delta x$. Those units are arbitrary motor positions and not calibrated. However i assume it as an proportional axis to the real displacement. For those fits I assume those values statically:
    \begin{align}
        T = 0\,\mathrm{K}\,, &&
        \gamma = 1\,\mathrm{neV}\,.
    \end{align}
    gap and noise cluster around:
    \begin{align}
        \Delta_0 = 188.5(5)\,\mathrm{ueV}\,, &&
        \sigma_V = 26(1)\,\mathrm{ueV}\,.
    \end{align}

    Figure \ref{fig:ac:pincode} summarizes the pincodes extracted from the complete opening trace. Configurations (a)--(c) belong to the high-conductance part of the opening and are separated by abrupt atomic rearrangements. Configuration (c) is carried by three resolved channels, with one dominant channel and two channels of comparable lower transmission. The transition to configuration (d) reduces the conductance to below $G_0$ and leaves two principal channels. A further rearrangement produces configuration (e), which is likewise dominated by two channels. After the final transition to configuration (f), the contact enters the tunnel regime and no finite-transmission channel is resolved by the MAR fit.

    The channel decomposition reveals information that is hidden in the total conductance. Within configurations (d) and (e), $G_\mathrm{N}$ increases gradually toward the end of each plateau. This increase is mainly carried by the most transmissive channel, while the transmission of the second channel decreases. The smooth redistribution within each labeled interval contrasts with the simultaneous jumps of several channels at its boundaries. I therefore associate the boundaries with changes of atomic configuration and the evolution within a configuration with continuous deformation of the contact.

    The complete measured transport landscape is shown in Fig.~\ref{fig:ac:pincode:cal}, and its reconstruction from the extracted pincodes is shown in Fig.~\ref{fig:ac:pincode:sim}.
