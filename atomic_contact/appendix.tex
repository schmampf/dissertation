\chapter{Supplementary: Atomic Contacts}
\label{appendix:ac}
%=========================================================

    This appendix complements the atomic-contact measurements in Sec.~\ref{sec:atomic-contacts} with the characterization data and extended model comparisons supporting the main analysis. I first characterize the intact constriction before breaking. Its hysteretic zero-field response and magnetic-field dependence establish the initial superconducting device state, which is distinct from the subsequently selected contact configurations C0--C3. I then introduce C0 in the tunnel regime. Static MAR fitting, the temperature dependence measured on the separate contact C0$'$, and microwave irradiation establish the superconducting energy scales and recover conventional photon-assisted quasiparticle tunneling while exposing the first weak finite-transmission correction.

    The following magnetic-field study compares C0--C3. It establishes a common empirical suppression scale near $28\,\mathrm{mT}$ and documents the configuration-dependent suppression of the superconducting features. I then provide the representative channel-resolved MAR fits and the complete measured and reconstructed transport landscapes underlying the mesoscopic-pincode analysis of the contact-opening trace.

    The final sections collect the extended microwave results. Tables summarize the static pincodes and coupling parameters of the measured configurations, while the C1 and C2 figures provide the charge-order decomposition, model-selection residuals, and complete measured and simulated photon-assisted transport landscapes. The appendix closes with the full measured and phenomenological landscapes of the high-conductance configuration C3. Together, these results document the parameter extraction, fit quality, model limits, and field dependence that support the more compact comparisons in the main text.

    \section{Characterization of the Unbroken Device}
    \label{sec:appendix:ac:unbroken}
    %=========================================================

        Before opening the break junction, I characterized the intact constriction in its unbroken state. This measurement establishes the electrical response of the device before mechanical adjustment and provides a reference for the subsequent contact configurations. I do not include the unbroken device in the C0--C3 series because its low resistance and hysteretic switching response place it in a distinct transport regime. The same device is denoted sample S2b in the detailed characterization by Raif \cite{raif_electronic_2024}.

The corresponding \textit{dV--dI} and \textit{dI--dV} traces emphasize the switching transitions and the finite slope of the low-voltage branch.

        Figure~\ref{fig:ac:magnetic-fields:unbroken:single-vi} shows the characteristic hysteresis of the intact constriction. On increasing the current magnitude, the device leaves the low-voltage branch at the switching current $I_\mathrm{sw}$. On the return sweep, it reenters this branch at the lower retrapping current $I_\mathrm{rt}$. Linear fits to the low-voltage and normal branches give the resistances $R_\mathrm{s}$ and $R_\mathrm{N}$, respectively. The extracted zero-field parameters are
        \begin{align}
            I_\mathrm{sw} = 1.075(5)\,\mathrm{mA}\,, &&
            I_\mathrm{rt} = 0.595(5)\,\mathrm{mA}\,, \\
            R_\mathrm{s} = 0.3387(2)\,\Omega\,, &&
            R_\mathrm{N} = 23.151(2)\,\Omega\,.
            \label{eq:ac:unbroken:transport}
        \end{align}

        The large separation between $R_\mathrm{s}$ and $R_\mathrm{N}$ identifies a well-resolved superconducting branch. The inequality $I_\mathrm{rt}<I_\mathrm{sw}$ quantifies the hysteresis. These values characterize the complete intact constriction and its measurement environment. They are not used as parameters for the channel-resolved transport models of the opened junction.

The small offset $\mu_0H_\mathrm{off}=0.5(1)\,\mathrm{mT}$ describes the displacement of the response along the field axis.

        I next applied a magnetic field perpendicular to the sample plane. Figure~\ref{fig:ac:magnetic-fields:unbroken} shows that the switching region narrows for both field polarities and is centered at a small field offset. I characterize this evolution by
        \begin{align}
            \mu_0H_\mathrm{c} = 9.9(1)\,\mathrm{mT}\,, &&
            \mu_0H_\mathrm{off} = 0.5(1)\,\mathrm{mT}\,.
            \label{eq:ac:unbroken:field}
        \end{align}
        Here, $\mu_0H_\mathrm{c}$ is the field scale at which the switching structure is no longer resolved and $\mu_0H_\mathrm{off}$ is the center of the measured field response. I treat $\mu_0H_\mathrm{c}$ as an empirical device parameter. The measurement does not distinguish orbital, geometric, and microscopic pair-breaking contributions.

        Figure~\ref{fig:ac:magnetic-fields:unbroken:landscape} resolves the same positive-field evolution in the complete transport traces. The low-voltage branch shortens with increasing field, while the switching and retrapping transitions move toward lower current magnitude. The complementary \textit{dI--dV} and \textit{dV--dI} peaks follow these boundaries and disappear as the field approaches $H_\mathrm{c}$. The agreement between the individual traces and the maps in Fig.~\ref{fig:ac:magnetic-fields:unbroken} shows that the extracted field scale is not tied to a single selected sweep.

        The unbroken constriction therefore behaves as a hysteretic superconducting weak link whose switching response is continuously suppressed by magnetic field. This characterization establishes the initial device state, but it does not determine the transport parameters of the junction after breaking. I therefore treat C0--C3 as separate configurations in the following sections.

    \section{Characterization of the Tunnel Regime}
    \label{appendix:ac:tunneling}
    %=========================================================

        \singlefitfigure{atomic_contact/0.05G0/15.0GHz_stripline/mar_fit}{
            Static transport of C0 in the tunnel regime. The multichannel MAR fit reproduces the measured \textit{I--V}, \textit{dI--dV}, and \textit{dV--dI} characteristics using the transmission eigenvalues in Eq.~\eqref{eq:ac:contact0:pincode}. The first-order BCS reference captures the dominant quasiparticle response but does not describe the weak $m=2$ MAR onset at $\lvert eV\rvert=\Delta_0$.
        }{fig:ac:contact0:mar-fit}

        I next opened the break junction and selected a low-conductance configuration denoted C0. This mechanical adjustment changes the device from the low-resistance, hysteretic weak link characterized above into a tunnel-regime junction. The static MAR fit shown in Fig.~\ref{fig:ac:contact0:mar-fit} gives
        \begin{align}
            \{\tau_i\} = \{0.045, 0.001, 0.001, 0.001, 0.001, 0.001\}\,, &&
            G_\mathrm{N} = 0.050\,G_0\,.
            \label{eq:ac:contact0:pincode}
        \end{align}
        Transport is therefore dominated by one weakly transmitting channel, and the first-order quasiparticle current provides the minimal description. C0 served as a tunnel-regime reference between the oxide tunnel junctions discussed earlier in Sec.~\ref{sec:tunnelbarrier} and the finite-transmission contacts analyzed in Sec.~\ref{subsec:ac:photon-assisted-transport}.

        The temperature sweep in Fig.~\ref{fig:ac:0.05G0:temperature} tests whether the dominant spectral structure follows the superconducting electrodes. At low temperature, the differential conductance is suppressed inside the gap and shows a pronounced quasiparticle onset near $2\Delta$. Increasing the temperature shifts spectral weight toward lower bias and weakens the onset as superconductivity is suppressed. The temperature dependence of C0$'$ and the magnetic-field-dependent characterization of C0 in Sec.~\ref{sec:appendix:ac:magnetic-field} give
        \begin{align}
            \Delta_0 = 192.0(5)\,\mathrm{ueV}\,, &&
            T_\mathrm{c} = 1.21(1)\,\mathrm{K}\,, &&
            \mu_0H_\mathrm{c} = 28(1)\,\mathrm{mT}\,.
        \end{align}
        Using the extracted gap for the weak-coupling relation gives $T_\mathrm{c}=1.24\,\mathrm{K}$. This value is close to the measured $1.21(1)\,\mathrm{K}$ and confirms that the temperature evolution of C0$'$ is consistent with the extracted superconducting gap.

        I then irradiated C0 through the stripline at $\nu=15.0\,\mathrm{GHz}$. Figure~\ref{fig:ac:0.05G0:15.0GHz_stripline:cal} shows replicas of the quasiparticle onset whose spacing and redistribution with drive amplitude follow Tien--Gordon photon-assisted tunneling. I calibrated the effective junction amplitude with the same procedure used in earlier in Sec.~\ref{sec:tunnelbarrier}.

        PAT simulations reproduces the principal quasiparticle sidebands and their transfer of spectral weight over the measured amplitude range. A weak measured subgap structure near the $n=2$ MAR threshold, is absent from the first-order tunnel calculation. This deviation is consistent with the onset of Andreev reflection from the leading channel. I do not resolve a separate photon-assisted MAR replica pattern in this contact, so the data do not justify a quantitative PAMAR analysis. The comparison consequently establishes the useful scope of the tunnel reference: Tien--Gordon dressing describes the dominant irradiated quasiparticle transport, while the remaining subgap signal marks the beginning of finite-transmission corrections.

        The tunnel-regime measurements therefore provide a control for the later finite-transmission analysis. They verify the superconducting energy scales through independent field and temperature sweeps, and they recover conventional photon-assisted quasiparticle tunneling under irradiation. At the same time, the weak subgap deviation shows why the tunnel model must be replaced by a channel-resolved MAR description as the contact transmission increases.

    \section{Magnetic-Field-Dependent Characterization}
    \label{sec:appendix:ac:magnetic-field}
    %=========================================================

        \begin{figure}[t]
            \centering
            \subfigure[\textit{dI--dV}]{\import{atomic_contact/magnetic-field}{dGexp.pgf}}
            \hfill
            \subfigure[\textit{dV--dI}]{\import{atomic_contact/magnetic-field}{dRexp.pgf}}
            \caption{
                Magnetic-field-dependent transport of C0--C3 for a field perpendicular to the sample plane. The maps show the normalized \textit{dI--dV} as a function of $eV/\Delta_0$ and the normalized \textit{dV--dI} as a function of $eI/(\Delta_0G_\mathrm{N})$. Each panel is labeled by its contact configuration, and both field polarities are shown.
                }
            \label{fig:ac:magnetic-fields}
        \end{figure}

        I measured C0--C3 as functions of magnetic field perpendicular to the sample plane. Figure~\ref{fig:ac:magnetic-fields} compares the resulting \textit{dI--dV} and \textit{dV--dI} maps. The center of the superconducting response occurs at a small configuration-dependent offset $\mu_0H_\mathrm{off}=0.5$--$1.5\,\mathrm{mT}$. I therefore characterize the suppression relative to the effective field $H-H_\mathrm{off}$. I attribute this small offset operationally to the residual field of the magnet and cryostat, without an independent calibration of its origin.

        Starting from $H_\mathrm{off}$, the superconducting transport features weaken with increasing field magnitude. In C0, the quasiparticle onset moves toward zero voltage and the subgap region fills as the gap closes. In C1--C3, the supercurrent contribution and subharmonic gap structure are progressively suppressed. Although the visible structures depend on the contact regime, they disappear at a relatively consistent field scale in both \textit{dI--dV} and \textit{dV--dI}. I define the empirical critical field as the field at which no resolved superconducting transport features remain. This criterion gives the common value
        \begin{align}
            \mu_0H_\mathrm{c} = 28.0(5)\,\mathrm{mT}\,.
            \label{eq:ac:magnetic-field:critical-field}
        \end{align}
        The uncertainty is given by the field resolution.

        Particularly noisy regions occur around $\mu_0H\approx\pm10\,\mathrm{mT}$ in the \textit{I--V} characteristics. These regions are noisy likely due to internal processes in the magnet powersupply or controller.

        A distinct abrupt change occurs in C3 for $\mu_0H\lesssim-20\,\mathrm{mT}$. In contrast to the continuously suppressed contribution, this transition changes the background conductance after the superconducting features have largely disappeared. I therefore attribute it tentatively to a reformation of the atomic contact. The data recorded before and after this event do not necessarily represent the same microscopic contact configuration. I consequently determine the superconducting suppression field from the continuous evolution outside this reformation event and do not interpret the conductance jump as superconducting hysteresis.

        Beyond the aptrupt change in conductance in C3 discussed before, there seem to be a different critical fields in the suppression of the zero voltage feature and the subgap structure (SGS). The SGS is supressed earlier and with that, the overall conductance gets lowered, while the supercurrent survives a higher field.

        The supercurrent contribution exhibits a pronounced difference between opposite bias-sweep directions. This behavior is consistent with slightly different switching and retrapping currents, $I_\mathrm{sw}>I_\mathrm{rt}$. It may indicate weakly hysteretic junction dynamics, but it does not by itself establish a departure from the overdamped limit.

        The similar suppression fields are consistent with a dominant field scale set by the common aluminum electrodes rather than by the individual channel transmissions. This conclusion remains empirical because I do not model the electrode geometry, orbital depairing, or microscopic pair breaking. The value in Eq.~\eqref{eq:ac:magnetic-field:critical-field} therefore denotes the disappearance of resolved superconducting transport features and should not be identified directly with an intrinsic bulk critical field.
