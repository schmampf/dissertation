\chapter{Superconducting Transport}
\label{ch:superconducting-transport}
%=========================================================

    We first identify the transport processes supported by a superconducting junction, then resolve their channel dependence at finite transmission, and finally determine how charging and the electromagnetic environment modify their dynamics. This sequence builds the description required for the experimental systems studied in this thesis, which combine mesoscopic transport with stochastic environmental effects.
    
    We begin in the weak transmission regime. Bardeen-Cooper-Schrieffer theory provides the quasiparticle spectrum and the basis for quasiparticle tunneling, while the Josephson description captures Cooper pair transport and phase dynamics. Both descriptions arise from the same superconducting foundation and expose complementary aspects of transport through a superconducting tunnel junction.

    We then extend the description to finite transmission, where the individual conduction channels and their transmission probabilities must be retained explicitly. This channel resolved treatment captures Andreev reflection, Andreev bound states, and multiple Andreev reflection. Finally, we embed the junction in its electromagnetic environment. Energy exchange with this environment and the energetic cost of charging determine the probability of individual charge transfer events. Together, these theoretical layers provide the framework for interpreting the measured \textit{I--V}, \textit{dI--dV}, and microwave driven transport features.

    Unless stated otherwise or varied explicitly, all numerical simulations in this chapter use aluminum parameters, zero temperature, and zero Dynes broadening, $T=\gamma=0$. Irradiated simulations use a drive frequency of $\nu=15\,\mathrm{GHz}$.
