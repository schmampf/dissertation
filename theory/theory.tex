\chapter{Superconducting Transport}
\label{ch:superconducting-transport}
%=========================================================
    The transport phenomena studied in this thesis arise from the interplay of superconductivity, mesoscopic junction transport, and environmental fluctuations. Their description therefore requires several complementary layers of theory. We first establish the superconducting foundation and distinguish the two transport contributions of a weakly coupled junction. Bardeen-Cooper-Schrieffer theory determines the quasiparticle spectrum and the resulting tunnel current, while the Josephson description captures coherent Cooper pair transport and phase dynamics.

    We then extend this foundation beyond the weak coupling limit. At finite transmission, transport depends on the individual conduction channels of the junction. Andreev reflection, Andreev bound states, and multiple Andreev reflection provide additional transport processes that are not contained in the lowest order tunnel description. This mesoscopic layer connects the superconducting properties of the electrodes to the channel structure of an atomic scale junction.

    Finally, we account for the fact that the junction is embedded in an electromagnetic environment. Charging effects, voltage and phase fluctuations, and energy exchange with the environment modify the underlying transfer processes and can make their dynamics stochastic. The resulting description is not separate from the microscopic and mesoscopic foundations. It uses their transport processes as inputs and describes how these processes occur in a fluctuating environment.

    The experimental systems considered in this thesis lie in this mesoscopic stochastic regime. Their transport can depend simultaneously on channel transmission, superconducting coherence, charging, and environmental fluctuations. The chapter therefore develops these theoretical layers in sequence before combining them in the interpretation of the measured \textit{I--V}, \textit{dI--dV}, and microwave driven transport features.

    Unless stated otherwise or varied explicitly, all numerical simulations in this chapter use aluminum parameters, zero temperature, and zero Dynes broadening, $T=\gamma=0$. Irradiated simulations use a drive frequency of $\nu=15\,\mathrm{GHz}$.
