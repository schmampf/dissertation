\chapter{Superconducting Transport}
\label{ch:superconducting-transport}
%=========================================================

    This chapter first identifies the transport processes supported by a superconducting junction, then resolves their channel dependence at finite transmission, and finally determines how charging and the electromagnetic environment modify their dynamics. This sequence builds the description required for the experimental systems studied in this thesis, which combine mesoscopic transport with stochastic environmental effects.
    
    The discussion begins in the weak transmission regime. Bardeen-Cooper-Schrieffer theory provides the quasiparticle spectrum and the basis for quasiparticle tunneling, while the Josephson description captures Cooper pair transport and phase dynamics. Both descriptions arise from the same superconducting foundation and expose complementary aspects of transport through a superconducting tunnel junction.

    At finite transmission, the individual conduction channels and their transmission probabilities must be retained explicitly. This channel resolved treatment captures Andreev reflection, Andreev bound states, and multiple Andreev reflection. Finally, we embed the junction in its electromagnetic environment. Energy exchange with this environment and the energetic cost of charging determine the probability of individual charge transfer events. Together, these theoretical layers provide the framework for interpreting the measured \textit{I--V}, \textit{dI--dV}, and microwave driven transport features.

    Unless stated otherwise or varied explicitly, all numerical simulations in this chapter use aluminum parameters, zero temperature, and zero Dynes broadening, $T=\gamma=0$. Irradiated simulations use a drive frequency of \qty{15}{\giga\hertz}.
